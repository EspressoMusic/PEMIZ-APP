# Product Store or Booking System: Which One Fits Your Business?

One of the first decisions when opening a digital business page is basic, but it shapes everything that follows: does your business sell a **product**, or deliver a **time-based service**? It sounds obvious, but it's worth pausing to define it correctly from the start.

## When a product store fits

A product store fits a business that sells something physical the customer picks, adds to a cart, and orders — with no dependency on a specific time. Typical examples: a bakery or pastry shop, a clothing store, jewelry, or any home business selling ready-made or made-to-order products. The customer sees a catalog, picks what they want, and orders.

## When a booking system fits

A booking system fits a business where the customer isn't buying a "thing" but a **slot of time** — a hair salon, skincare studio, therapy, consulting. Here the focus is an availability calendar: when there's an open slot, and when there isn't. There's no product "inventory," just an inventory of hours.

## What both types have in common

Despite the difference, both paths share the same core idea:

- **One business page** with a name, description, and contact details.
- **One shareable link** across every channel your customers use.
- **One dashboard** where you see orders, bookings, and customer messages — regardless of store type.

That means the choice between a product store and a booking system doesn't change the day-to-day management experience — it only determines how the customer experiences the page: a catalog to choose from, or a calendar to book.

## You can change it later

There's no need to fear getting the initial choice wrong. Businesses evolve — a salon that starts selling skincare products too, or a product business that adds paid personal consulting. The initial choice is a starting point, not a life sentence. The page's job is to reflect the business as it is today, and adapt as it changes.
