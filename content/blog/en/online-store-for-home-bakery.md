# How to Open an Online Store for a Home Bakery or Food Business

Home food businesses — bakers, pastry makers, home chefs — face a slightly different challenge than other businesses: the product changes from order to order, there's strong seasonality around holidays and events, and orders almost always need to be arranged in advance. An organized online store can make all of that much simpler, for both the customer and the business owner.

## What's different about selling food online

Unlike an "off the shelf" product, homemade food often requires personalization — flavor, quantity, design for an event. It matters that the customer can clearly see what's available to order, in which variations, and at what price range — so they don't arrive with expectations that don't match what the business actually offers.

Quality photos matter more here than almost anywhere else: a customer ordering a birthday cake wants to see exactly what to expect.

## Managing pre-orders and a scheduling window

A large share of home food businesses don't operate "here and now" — they work on pre-orders, sometimes days or weeks ahead, especially around holidays. You can set a fixed window during which orders are accepted, so customers don't try to order at the last minute for a date that's no longer realistic to prepare for. This also prevents unpredictable overload on the busiest days of the year.

## What it looks like in practice

- Upload your regular products (cakes, pastries, gift boxes) with photos and prices.
- Add special promotions ahead of holidays and events.
- Share the store link with existing customers and in your Instagram bio.
- Orders land in one dashboard, with the option to approve each order before it moves into production.

That way, even during the busiest seasons — Passover, the New Year, weekend birthdays — there's one clear place that centralizes everything ordered, instead of trying to remember who asked for what from a long message thread.

## Fixed products or a changing menu?

A home food store is a classic example of a product store rather than a booking system, since the customer chooses an item, not a slot of time. If you're still deciding between store types, [Product Store or Booking System](/en/blog/product-store-vs-appointments) helps clarify the difference.
